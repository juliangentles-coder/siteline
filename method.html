The credibility section

How this is measured — and what it isn't

The same honesty that makes a good backtest useful applies here: plain about method, plain about limits. Read this before you buy, not after.

How these were measured

  • Aerial video captured over public streets, framed at an oblique angle — steep enough to separate objects, shallow enough for the detector to recognise them.
  • Objects detected and tracked frame to frame, so each subject is counted once as it crosses the line. The line coordinates are printed on every card in the data.
  • People and vehicles are counted in aggregate only. No faces, no plates, no identification of anyone.
  • Confidence is the detector's mean score across the clip. Above 0.5 is usable; lower means objects were small or partly hidden and the count runs conservative.

What these numbers are not

  • Not multi-day averages. Each is a single sample of two to three minutes, projected to an hour for comparison.
  • Not certified counts. Good estimates for a decision, not court-defensible traffic studies.
  • Not directly comparable between sites without care — framing, angle and time of day differ. Same-site comparisons over time are the reliable ones.
  • Dense crowds run under, not over: overlapping people merge and go uncounted.

Privacy & legality

  • No identification, ever. Footage is framed and altitude-set for counting, not recognition — faces and plates are not resolvable in the source video, and only aggregate counts leave the pipeline.
  • Public rights-of-way only. Capture happens over public streets and from rooftop vantage points, not inside private units or through windows.
  • Drone flights follow Transport Canada's RPAS rules for the applicable operating category; rooftop-tripod holds require no flight at all.
  • Your footage, on request. The annotated clip behind every count is available to the client — nothing is packaged or resold without the site owner's knowledge.
